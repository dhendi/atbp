Business registration
Every PH payment gateway requires this before it will release your live API keys — it's the actual bottleneck, budget a few days for approval. Also the prerequisite for Barangay Clearance → Mayor's Permit → BIR registration below (see Legal & compliance).
DTI (sole proprietor)₱200 – ₱2,000
SEC (corporation)₱5,000 – ₱10,000+

If registering as a DTI sole proprietor (the simpler, more common path for a solo founder)

  • One valid government ID (passport, driver's license, UMID, PhilSys/National ID, etc.)
  • Your proposed business name — check availability first on the DTI site; have 2–3 backups ready
  • Business address and territorial scope (barangay / city / regional / national — this sets the fee)
  • TIN, if you already have one from prior employment or a previous BIR registration
  • Registration fee, paid online
If incorporating instead (SEC — a corporation or One Person Corporation), the document set is materially different and more involved: Articles of Incorporation, a Treasurer's Affidavit, proof of paid-up capital, and (for an OPC) a nominee and alternate nominee. Worth a lawyer or accountant for that path rather than DIY.
Business bank account
A separate, easy-to-miss bottleneck: banks require your DTI/SEC certificate, Mayor's Permit, and BIR Certificate of Registration before they'll open one — so it can't happen until those are done, and it in turn gates both the payment gateway's live-key approval and being able to pay sellers by anything other than your personal account. Budget 1–2 weeks, and start it the moment the Mayor's Permit clears rather than after.
BDO, BPI, Metrobank, UnionBank, etc.Varies — initial deposit + maintaining balance

Typically required (confirm with your chosen bank — requirements do vary)

  • DTI Certificate of Registration (or SEC Certificate of Incorporation)
  • Mayor's / Business Permit
  • BIR Certificate of Registration (Form 2303)
  • Barangay Business Clearance
  • Two valid government IDs of the account signatory/ies
  • Initial deposit (varies by bank, often ₱5,000–₱25,000+ for a business account)
  • Company/business seal or stamp, if the bank requires one

Compliance, not code

These don't live in the repo, but a payment processor or a lawyer will ask about them before real transactions flow.

Barangay Business Clearance From the barangay of your registered business address — a prerequisite for the Mayor's Permit below, not optional even for an online-only business tied to a home address. Rules on how strictly this is enforced for digital-only operations vary by LGU — confirm with your barangay directly.

Typically required

  • DTI/SEC Certificate of Registration
  • Valid government ID of the owner/applicant
  • Proof of business address (lease contract; or if home-based, a utility bill plus the property owner's written consent if you're not the owner)
  • Community Tax Certificate (Cedula) — get this at the barangay/city hall if you don't have one
  • Completed barangay clearance application form (provided at the barangay hall)
  • Clearance fee (varies by barangay, commonly a few hundred pesos)
Mayor's / Business Permit Issued by the City or Municipal Hall of your registered address, after the Barangay Clearance. Required before BIR registration.

Typically required — varies more by LGU than any other item here

  • Barangay Business Clearance
  • DTI/SEC Certificate of Registration
  • Community Tax Certificate (Cedula)
  • Contract of Lease (if renting) or Tax Declaration/proof of ownership (if the address is owned)
  • Locational/Zoning Clearance — some LGUs require this even for a home-based online business; ask your city's Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO) directly, since this is the detail most likely to surprise you
  • Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (Bureau of Fire Protection) — requirements for a purely online, non-customer-facing business vary by city; confirm before assuming it's waived
  • 1x1 or 2x2 ID photos, application form (from the BPLO)
  • Permit fee — based on declared capitalization or gross receipts, varies significantly by LGU
BIR registration Required once you're operating as a real business collecting payment — tax registration and official receipts. Comes after the Mayor's Permit.

Typically required — confirm current specifics with your RDO or an accountant; BIR rules for this have genuinely changed recently

  • BIR Form 1901 (sole proprietor/self-employed) or Form 1903 (corporation/partnership)
  • DTI Certificate of Registration (or SEC Certificate of Incorporation)
  • Mayor's / Business Permit (or the official receipt for a pending application — some RDOs accept this)
  • Valid government ID
  • Proof of business address
  • Registration of Books of Accounts (manual, loose-leaf, or a Computerized Accounting System)
  • Registration/Authority to Print for your official receipts or invoices, or enrollment in BIR's e-invoicing system if applicable to a platform business
The old ₱500 Annual Registration Fee (BIR Form 0605) was removed under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act (RA 11976) in 2024 — don't budget for it, but do confirm with your RDO, since this is exactly the kind of detail that changes and easily goes stale in any generic checklist, including this one.
RA 11967 platform registration The Terms, Privacy Policy, Buyer Protection page, and the dispute/redress flow already commit to specific obligations under the Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) — but the platform itself still needs to register with DTI as an online sales platform/e-marketplace under that law. Separate from plain DTI business-name registration above.
This is a newer (2023) law with an evolving IRR — the exact registration process and required documents for an "Online Sales Platform" specifically (as distinct from an individual online seller) is the one item on this whole list worth a direct call to DTI or a lawyer rather than guessing from general knowledge.
NPC registrationThe National Privacy Commission requires registering data processing systems once you're handling personal and payment data at scale. Free to file, easy to forget. The incident-response half of this is already done — see docs/internal/data-breach-response.md — but that internal procedure still needs a lawyer's sign-off on its NPC-notification timing before relying on it for real.
Seller KYCThe admin approval queue (seller status: pending → approved) already exists in the app — decide what documentation you'll actually check before approving someone.
