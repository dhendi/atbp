Part A — Understanding ATBP
A1

Brand & business overview

What you're actually operating, in plain terms.

What ATBP is

ATBP ("at iba pa" — Filipino for "and other things," "and so on") is a Philippine online marketplace built around handmade, vintage, pre-loved, collectible, and made-to-order goods, plus a small set of adjacent formats: shelf-stable food & snacks, remote services, digital products, closets (personal pre-loved selling), yard sales (time-boxed clear-outs), and real-world maker markets/events. The product positioning is deliberately "weekend bazaar," not "everything store" — the homepage and Explore page are built around discovery (Browse by Vibe, Trending, curated Picks) rather than a single dense search grid.

What ATBP is not

  • Not a first-party retailer. ATBP does not buy, hold, or resell inventory — every listing belongs to an independent seller.
  • Not (yet) a marketplace with a live payment gateway. Every payment method today — GCash, Maya, QR Ph, Card, Online Banking, COD — resolves through a mock provider that always "succeeds." No real money moves through Xendit, PayMongo, or any card processor at this time. This is the single most important operating fact in this handbook — see the callout below.
  • Not a mass-market commodity marketplace competing purely on price and catalogue size (that's Shopee/Lazada/TikTok Shop's game — see the Seller Guide's competitor comparison for how ATBP is positioned against them).
Operating reality: ATBP's payments, payouts, and seller subscription billing are all currently mocked (see src/lib/payments/provider.ts, src/lib/payouts/provider.ts). Every order "succeeds" instantly in code. This is fine for demonstrating the product and onboarding early sellers under manual/off-platform settlement, but no admin should assume real money is moving until a live PSP (Xendit is the name referenced in the schema, though no integration code exists yet) is actually wired in. Treat every payment-related admin action in this handbook as correct in structure, not yet live in effect.

Target sellers

Handmade makers and artists; collectors (trading cards, toys, anime/gaming, vinyl); vintage and pre-loved sellers; closet and yard-sale sellers; small local businesses and brands; made-to-order/custom creators; approved shelf-stable food & snack sellers; freelancers offering remote services; digital-product creators.

Target buyers

People browsing for something specific and interesting rather than searching a commodity catalogue: collectors, gift shoppers, pre-loved/thrift shoppers, fans of Filipino makers, local-discovery shoppers, and value-conscious shoppers checking Deals.

Marketplace categories (top-level)

Handmade & Art · Vintage & Pre-Loved · Collectibles & Hobbies · Fashion & Accessories · Home & Living · Filipino Finds · Tech & Gadgets · Baby, Kids & Pets · Automotive & Outdoor · Party & Events · Food & Snacks · Services · Digital Products — 13 parent groups over roughly 30 leaf categories (prisma/seed.ts, managed at /admin/categories).

Marketplace concepts & terminology

The vocabulary the rest of this handbook assumes you know.

Closets — a seller's personal pre-loved selling format, distinct from their main shop; browsable at /closets.
Yard Sales — time-boxed clear-out events with a start/end date; auto-archived once endDate passes.
Made-to-Order — listings built after the buyer orders, tagged via the madeToOrder attribute.
Drops — scheduled product releases that flip from UPCOMING to LIVE automatically at a set time.
ATBP Picks — hand-curated Collection spotlights, deliberately not algorithmic.
Trending — fully algorithmic (view/save/cart/bid/purchase-weighted, 7-day window); no admin override exists.
Deals — a seller-set scheduled sale price/window on their own listing; no admin deals tool exists.
Tawad — the platform's buyer-initiated offer/counter-offer mechanism on eligible listings.
Founding Seller — one of the first 200 approved, BIR-verified business sellers; a permanent, one-time grant.
SellerPlanOverride — an admin-grantable exception to a seller's listing/auction limits — not a commission-rate override.
Buyer Protection — an opt-in, buyer-paid fee that backs a platform-funded refund guarantee on a dispute.
GMV (as currently computed) — sum of Order.total for every non-cancelled order. Note this includes shipping/fees and isn't scoped to completed orders — a looser figure than "true" GMV.

B2

Seller lifecycle & trust levers

Approval → verification → badges → warnings → suspension.

1. Approval — /admin/sellers

What it does: moves a new seller from PENDING to APPROVED, notifies the seller and nearby users, and may trigger a Founding Seller grant if the seller is already BIR-verified. Who uses it: any admin. Check before approving: shop name/handle aren't impersonating an existing brand; description and category are coherent; if claiming Business status, that a BIR number is at least present. What can go wrong: approving a seller who immediately lists prohibited items — mitigated by the 3-strike warning system below, not prevented up front.

2. BIR verification — verifyBirLicenseAction

Manual admin attestation, business sellers only, requires a birRegistrationNumber on file. Sets birVerified = true, auto-upgrades the seller to the BIR_VERIFIED plan if they're still on Free, and may trigger the Founding Seller grant. Requires legal/policy review: the codebase does not verify the BIR number against any government API — this is a manual, honor-adjacent check today. Treat any BIR number as unverified against source until a real registry lookup exists.

3. Badges — /admin/verification

Twelve toggleable badges: Verified Seller, Verified Maker, Verified Pre-Loved, Authenticated, Rising Seller, Highly Rated, Fast Shipper, Local Seller, Physical Store, Founding Seller, Sales 100, Sales 1000. Toggling any badge on sets the seller's general verified flag true. Best practice: badges are a trust signal buyers see on every product card — don't grant "Verified" badges without at least confirming the seller has fulfilled real orders.

4. Warnings & the 3-strike rule

flagProhibitedItemAction(productId, reason) removes the listing, logs a SellerWarning, notifies the seller, and automatically suspends the seller once they reach 3 active warnings (AUTO_SUSPEND_WARNING_COUNT = 3), revoking their sessions. clearSellerWarningAction deactivates one warning without deleting its history — use this to correct a mistaken flag, not to reset the count to give a seller a "clean slate."

5. Manual suspend / reinstate

Independent of the 3-strike system — an admin can suspend or reinstate a seller directly for cause. Reinstate only works from a SUSPENDED status. Escalate to founder (Medium): any suspension of a seller with meaningful order volume or a public dispute already in progress.

Recommended future admin tool: a direct plan-assignment action. Today, an admin cannot put a specific seller onto PRO or PREMIUM directly — SellerPlanOverride only adjusts listing/auction limits, never the commission percentage or plan tier itself. The only way a seller's plan actually changes is self-serve (changeSellerPlanAction) or the automatic BIR_VERIFIED assignment.

B3

Product moderation & reports

Two entry points, one shared enforcement pipeline.

Direct moderation — /admin/products

Cycle any product between Active, Flagged, and Removed. This is a status flip only — there is no admin capability to edit a listing's title, price, description, or images. If a listing is wrong rather than prohibited (e.g. miscategorized, obviously mispriced by a typo), the current options are Remove-and-ask-the-seller-to-relist, or leave it — there's no in-between "correct it" action.

Prohibited-item flagging — the real enforcement path

flagProhibitedItemAction is what should be used for anything violating the Prohibited Items policy (weapons, drugs, counterfeits, unregistered medicines, live animals, regulated chemicals, IP infringement, unauthorized digital-content resale, academic dishonesty for hire, unlicensed professional advice). It requires a reason, removes the listing, and feeds the 3-strike system (B2). Always use this path, not the plain status toggle, for anything that's actually a policy violation — only the flagging path creates the accountability trail.

Reports queue — /admin/reports

Report targetHandling
Product (listing reports)Grouped by product, sorted by report count then recency. confirmListingViolationAction routes into the same flag/warn/auto-suspend pipeline above; dismissListingReportsAction clears them with no effect on the listing.
Seller / Livestream / UserIndividually actioned — Mark Reviewed, Resolve, or Dismiss. These do not themselves remove anything or warn anyone; use the seller/product actions above for the actual enforcement, and this queue to track that you looked at it.

Resolving or dismissing any report notifies the original reporter in-app — buyers can see their report wasn't ignored.

What should escalate to the founder (Medium/High): any report alleging counterfeit goods from a Verified/Highly-Rated seller, any report involving a minor, any report alleging an off-platform scam that could implicate ATBP's own liability, and any single listing that accumulates 3+ independent reports before an admin has looked at it.

Part B — The Admin Dashboard
B4

Orders, payments, payouts & disputes

The money path — and where admin control actually stops.

Orders — read-only

/admin/orders lists the last 100 orders with status badges (PAYMENT_PENDING → PROCESSING → SHIPPED/IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED → COMPLETED, with CANCELLED/DISPUTED side-states). There is no admin action to directly change an order's status. The only admin-side lever that moves an order's status is resolving a Dispute (below).

Payments — 100% mocked, confirm before relying on this in production

Every PaymentMethodId — GCash, Maya, QR Ph, Card, Online Banking, COD — resolves through MockPaymentProvider, which returns success immediately (COD returns "pending," deferred to delivery). No Xendit, PayMongo, or card-processor integration exists. Planned / Future Feature

Payouts — self-serve, automated, mocked

Sellers request payouts themselves from Studio against their available balance (completed-order commissions minus payouts already committed). Standard payout → PROCESSING; instant payout (flat ₱99 fee) → PAID immediately. /admin/transactions is read-only — there is no admin queue to approve, reject, or intervene in a payout. Once a real disbursement provider is wired in, this will need a real failure-handling admin surface; today, a "failed" payout in the mock world essentially can't happen.

Disputes — the actual refund/return mechanism

ATBP has no separate "returns" flow — everything routes through Dispute. Lifecycle: OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_REFUND | RESOLVED_DENIED | CLOSED. The admin UI at /admin/disputes only exposes two buttons: Approve Refund and Deny — there's no UI to move a dispute into Under Review or Closed, even though the underlying action supports it.

  • Approve Refund → sets Order.status = CANCELLED, paymentStatus = REFUNDED. No real refund transaction fires (consistent with the mock payment layer) — this is a status flip, and it will need to trigger a real reversal once payments are live.
  • Deny → sets Order.status = COMPLETED.
  • The dispute page shows a Protected / Not protected badge based on whether the buyer paid the Buyer Protection Fee. A refund on a "Not protected" order has no platform fund behind it — in practice, that recovery would have to come from the seller, not ATBP.
Legal grounding: ATBP's Terms of Service §8 (Redress Mechanism and Dispute Resolution) commits to acknowledging and acting on a complaint promptly, and — consistent with the Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) — tells users they may escalate to the DTI's E-Commerce Bureau if a complaint isn't resolved within 7 calendar days. Treat 7 days as the hard ceiling for getting any dispute to at least UNDER_REVIEW in practice, even though the UI doesn't enforce that state today.

Part B — The Admin Dashboard
B5

Pricing, plans & promotions

One page, three distinct pricing systems.

Seller plans

/admin/pricing edits SellerPlan rows directly — monthly price, commission %, listing/auction limits, feature flags — for Free, Pro, Premium, BIR_VERIFIED, and Founding Premium. Any change here retroactively affects every seller currently on that plan (the rate is read live via getEffectiveLimits, not locked in until an order is placed — at which point recordCommission bakes that rate into the order's Commission row permanently). Escalate to founder before changing any live plan's commission % or price — this is a company-wide pricing decision, not a routine admin task.

Per-seller overrides

SellerPlanOverride lets an admin grant one seller extra listing/auction headroom without changing their plan or commission — the "reward a trusted seller" lever. Requires a free-text reason (visible in the audit log).

Seller-purchased promotions

Sellers buy Boost/Featured/Category Feature/Homepage Feature/Sponsored Campaign placements themselves (buyPromotionAction), paid via the mock provider or a PromotionalCredit ledger. Admin's role is pricing only — editing each PromotionType's min/max price band and active flag. Admin cannot create, pause, or cancel an individual seller's promotion.

Third-party advertising

Fully admin-created at /admin/advertising — one form creates the advertiser, campaign, ad creative, and placement(s) (Homepage/Category/Search/Seller Page/Event Page) together. Every placement is labeled "Sponsored" on the storefront. Gap: no edit, pause, or delete action exists for a live ad — if a campaign needs to stop early, there is currently no UI lever to do it.

Auctions — built, zero admin surface, currently off

AUCTIONS_ENABLED = false in src/lib/feature-flags.ts, gating auctions everywhere including all discovery feeds. The stated internal rationale is to wait for a critical mass of buyers (rough target: ~100k active buyers) before bidding will feel alive rather than half-dead. Even when re-enabled, there is no /admin/auctions route — an admin cannot currently cancel a stuck auction, adjust its end time, or intervene in a bidding dispute by any dedicated tool.

Part B — The Admin Dashboard
B6

Content, categories & local

Curation, taxonomy, and real-world presence.

ATBP Picks (Collections)

Deliberately hand-curated, not rule-based. Create a collection (slug/title/subtitle/emoji, type Pick/Seasonal/Shops), then add products by exact title search (must resolve to exactly one match) or sellers by @handle. Toggle active/hidden, or delete. These surface on Explore and /picks. Best practice: refresh at least one active collection weekly — a stale "ATBP Picks" shelf undercuts the platform's curated-discovery pitch.

Categories

Create a new top-level category (icon emoji + name, auto-slugified) or delete one (blocked while any product still references it). There's no nesting UI — to make a category a child of another, an admin currently has to edit its parentId directly in the database. There's no rename/re-icon action either — a wrong category name today means delete-and-recreate (only possible once nothing references it), not edit.

Category suggestions

Sellers can submit freeform "Other" tags. Approving one at /admin/category-suggestions is informational only — it does not create a real category. If a suggestion is worth acting on, an admin still has to separately go create it at Categories.

Markets & Events

Markets are simple directory entries (name, city, tagline, image, schedule) with no seller/vendor relationship — create, toggle active, delete. Events are richer real-world community events (date, venue, address, admission, categories, organiser) that sellers can self-join or leave, with a status control (Upcoming/Live/Ended/Cancelled) and automatic nearby-user notification on creation. Event-specific promotions share the same pricing table as product promotions but have no dedicated buy/manage UI beyond the shared price-band editor.

Local

"Local" (nearby sellers, nearby products, area selection) is entirely seller- and buyer-managed — there is no admin oversight page for local listings specifically. If a local-area issue comes up (e.g. a seller misrepresenting their location), it routes through the general Seller support or Reports process, not a dedicated local-admin tool.

Daily admin checklist

A daily maintenance cron also runs automatically at 18:00 UTC (Vercel Hobby plan — once/day, ±59 min slop) covering 10 sweeps: settling expired auctions, notifying started/ending-soon auctions, activating scheduled Drops, expiring overdue Yard Sales, expiring stale Tawad offers, pruning old trending events, auto-confirming overdue shipments/service orders, releasing overdue digital-product holds, and settling expired promotions. The checklist below is what still needs a human.

Every morning

New orders since last check
New seller applications (Pending)
Open Reports queue
Open Disputes queue
New Support tickets
Anything flagged overnight by users
Active Advertising campaigns still look right

During the day

Seller support requests
Buyer support requests
New listing reports as they arrive
Prohibited-item flags needing action
Anything a Medium/High escalation (see E2)

End of day

Any dispute still Open past its 7-day clock
Seller issues left unresolved
Today's GMV / order count (dashboard)
Outstanding support tickets carried to tomorrow
Anything escalated to the founder today — confirm it landed
There is currently no admin-facing fraud/failed-payment alert to check each morning, because payments are mocked and cannot fail in the way a live gateway's would. Once a real PSP is wired in, add "failed payments" and "fraud indicators" as their own daily line items — they're absent from this list on purpose, not by oversight.

Weekly admin checklist

A wider lens than the daily pass — mostly manual today, since no weekly-report tool exists yet.

Seller activity. New sellers approved this week; any pending applications aging past a few days.

Marketplace liquidity. Rough gut-check: are there enough active listings in the categories buyers are actually browsing (Trending, Deals)? No liquidity metric is computed automatically.

C3

Monthly business review

What ATBP actually measures today, versus what a growing marketplace eventually needs.

Computed today, on the /admin dashboard

MetricWhat it means here
GMVSum of Order.total for every non-cancelled order — includes shipping/fees, not scoped to Completed only. Directional, not precise.
Total orders / users / sellersSimple counts. "Total sellers" counts Approved only; "Pending sellers" tracked separately.
Open reports / open disputesA live backlog gauge — rising counts are an early warning before they become support-ticket volume.
14-day revenue seriesDaily GMV for the trailing 14 days — the only trend chart that exists.

Requested metrics not currently computed — Recommended Future Admin Tools

Average Order Value (AOV), buyer/seller retention and cohort analysis, buyer/seller acquisition cost, refund rate, cancellation rate, seller sell-through rate, seller-by-revenue leaderboards, and category-level breakdowns are not computed anywhere in the codebase today. Each is derivable from existing data (Order, Commission, Dispute, SellerProfile rows) but requires new queries/reports to be built — do not assume a number for any of these exists to pull from the admin dashboard.

Chargeback rate specifically has no underlying concept to measure yet — there is no chargeback handling anywhere in the code, tracking with there being no live card processor to originate one from.

What this means for now: a real monthly business review currently requires an admin (or a developer) to run direct database queries for anything beyond the dashboard's headline numbers. Treat "define and build the missing metrics" itself as a founder-level product priority once monthly reviews become a standing habit.

Part D — Support & Trust
D1

Seller support playbook

Ten situations, and the actual lever available for each.

SituationWhat to actually do
Seller can't list / hit their limitCheck their plan's maxActiveListings/maxNewListingsPerMonth against current usage. If they've genuinely earned more headroom without upgrading, grant a SellerPlanOverride with a reason; otherwise point them at Studio → Plan & Billing to upgrade.
Seller can't receive a payoutCheck SellerProfile.status — payouts are blocked outright if suspended. Otherwise check their wallet's availableBalance; a payout request over that amount is rejected by design.
Seller wants to edit an orderNo admin lever exists. Orders are read-only in /admin/orders. Route this through the seller's own order tools, or a Dispute if it's a genuine problem needing resolution.
Seller wants to cancel an orderNo direct "cancel" admin action exists outside Disputes. If warranted, treat it as a dispute and resolve accordingly.
Seller receives a buyer complaintCheck whether a Report or Dispute has actually been filed — a complaint alone (e.g. via messaging, which is not admin-visible) doesn't create either automatically.
Seller disputes a refund decisionReview the Dispute's evidence and the Protected/Not-protected badge before deciding — there's no appeal workflow beyond re-opening the conversation manually; escalate to founder if the seller pushes back hard (Medium).
Seller suspects buyer fraudNo buyer-fraud-specific tool exists. Use Reports (target type User) and manual judgment; suspend the buyer's account via /admin/users only for clear cause.
Seller wants to close their storeNo graceful self-serve or admin "close shop" action exists — only Suspend, which reads as punitive. Handle manually: confirm no pending orders, then suspend and note the reason as voluntary closure so it doesn't count against future re-review.
Seller wants to upgrade/downgradeSelf-serve via Studio → Plan & Billing (changeSellerPlanAction). Admin has no direct plan-assignment lever (see B2).
Seller asks about their commission rateConfirm their current plan and its published rate (10% Free/Pro, 8% Premium/Founding). Admin cannot grant a custom rate outside the plan system.

D2

Buyer support playbook

Ten situations, and where they actually route today.

SituationWhat to actually do
Order not receivedCheck the shipment's status/tracking (if a real courier is ever active) or the order's status. If past a reasonable window, this is a Dispute.
Wrong item receivedDispute — the only refund/return mechanism that exists.
Item arrived damagedDispute, same as above. Ask for photos before approving a refund.
Seller isn't respondingBuyer-seller messaging has no admin visibility today — you cannot check whether a seller actually saw a message. Rely on the buyer's account of the timeline and the order's own status/age.
Refund requestDispute → Approve Refund or Deny. Check the Protected/Not-protected badge first (see B4) — it changes who ultimately bears the cost.
Payment problemToday, "payment problem" almost always means a UI/flow question, not a real gateway failure — all payment methods are mocked and effectively cannot fail. Once a live PSP exists, this line item needs a real triage path.
Account problem (login, profile)/admin/users only supports Suspend/Reinstate — there's no admin edit of a buyer's name/email/password. Direct password issues to the standard reset flow.
Suspicious sellerReport (target Seller) + manual review of their listings/warning history.
Suspicious productReport (target Product) — feeds the listing-reports queue in B3.
ChargebackNot applicable — no card-network chargeback concept exists without a live processor.
Wants to cancel before shipmentNo buyer-side or admin-side direct cancel exists outside a Dispute — treat a clean pre-shipment cancellation request as a straightforward Approve Refund.

Part E — Business & Escalation
E1

Business operations

The economics, restated for operating decisions — not just the revenue list in A2.

Marketplace economics, in one paragraph

ATBP earns primarily from the 10%/8% commission on completed sales, topped up by seller subscriptions (₱699–1,999/mo) for sellers who want more listings and tools, plus smaller flat fees (₱15 processing, ₱99 instant payout, the buyer-paid Buyer Protection Fee) and self-serve seller promotions. None of this requires ATBP to hold inventory or take on merchandising risk — the core cost structure is platform infrastructure (hosting, database, cron, storage) plus people (support, moderation, engineering), not cost of goods.

Costs to monitor as the business grows

  • Infrastructure scaling. Database connections, image/blob storage, and background cron capacity all scale with GMV and listing count — watch these against the hosting plan's limits well before they become an outage.
  • Support headcount vs. ticket volume. With no chat-content moderation tooling and read-only reviews, Trust & Safety work is currently more manual than it should be at scale (see D3) — this is a real cost driver, not just a feature gap.
  • Founding Seller cost. The 8% rate + free year of Pro for up to 200 sellers is a real forgone-revenue cost, sized and capped by design (FOUNDING_SELLER_LIMIT = 200) — worth tracking claimed slots against the runway it was budgeted for.
  • Payment-gateway integration cost. Whenever a live PSP (Xendit or otherwise) is wired in, budget for its own transaction fees on top of ATBP's own commission — these are two separate costs that shouldn't be confused with each other when pricing is reviewed.

Future monetization opportunities — not yet implemented

A formalized third-party advertising rate card (impressions/CPC billing terms), tiered/featured seller placement subscriptions beyond the current promotion price bands, and any B2B/API revenue are all plausible extensions of what already exists in the schema, but none are built or priced today — do not present any of these as active revenue.

Three tiers. When in doubt, escalate a level.

LowAny admin, same dayRoutine seller approval; a single dismissible report; a normal dispute with clear evidence either way; a standard support ticket.

Recommended future admin tools

Every gap flagged in this handbook, gathered in one place.

  1. Live payment gateway integration (Xendit or equivalent) — the single highest-priority item; everything downstream of payments is mocked until this exists.
  2. Review moderation — hide/delete/flag actions for /admin/reviews, currently fully read-only.
  3. Admin visibility into buyer-seller messaging — at minimum a read-only escalation view for Trust & Safety investigations.
  4. Direct seller plan assignment — let an admin place a specific seller onto a plan without relying on self-serve or the BIR auto-upgrade path.
  5. Order status control for admins — a supervised way to correct an order's status without routing everything through Disputes.
  6. Advertisement edit/pause/delete — the schema already supports Paused/Ended states; no UI reaches them.
  7. A graceful "close my store" flow, distinct from Suspend, for sellers leaving voluntarily.
  8. Category rename/re-icon and a real nesting UI — today both require direct database edits.
  9. Payout failure handling — trivial today because payouts are mocked; will need a real admin queue once a live disbursement provider exists.
  10. Missing monthly-review metrics — AOV, retention/cohort analysis, CAC, refund/cancellation rate, seller-by-revenue leaderboards, category breakdowns (see C3).
  11. A formal SLA/escalation timer on Reports and Disputes, rather than the flat manual queues that exist today — useful for holding the Terms' 7-day redress commitment consistently as volume grows.
  12. Auctions admin UI — needed before auctions are re-enabled, since none exists even in the built feature.
None of the above are missing because of an oversight worth worrying about today — ATBP is early, and this list is exactly what "early" looks like. Treat it as a prioritization input, not a criticism of the current build.
